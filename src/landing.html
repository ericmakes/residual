UGC ads where the creator is paid on ad spend they can see.

Brands pay creators a share of what they spend running the video. Residual pulls that spend from Meta, TikTok and Google every morning, line by line, and both sides read the same ledger.

Brands pay nothing on their first $10,000 of spend, then 0.75% capped at $5,000 a month; Trybe charges 1.25% from the first dollar. Creators pay 5% of what they earn and are paid every Friday. No Shopify needed, which is the point: apps, subscriptions, fintech and regulated advertisers have been locked out of this model so far.

Why this exists

A note from the person building it.

Trybe got one thing right and it is worth copying: brands pay creators on results rather than per video, and creators who make ads that run get paid for as long as they run. Five hundred of Trybe's brands pay a straight percentage of ad spend, most of them 10%, according to the rates Trybe publishes. That is a real market, and it is bigger than the Shopify stores Trybe built it for.

The problem is what the creator gets to see. A statement with a total on it. No spend, no ad IDs, no way to check the number against anything. And a video a brand approves but never bothers to launch earns nothing, which on a commission-only rule is a quiet way of getting free content. I run paid media for app and betting brands in Latin America, and no finance team I have worked with would pay a vendor invoice with that little behind it. Creators accept it because nobody has offered them better, at least so far.

So Residual pays on the number the ad platform reports for the ad IDs tied to the video, and shows the creator the same lines the brand sees. Approval starts a 21-day clock: run the video to $50 of spend or the approval lapses, the video goes back to the creator, and the lapse goes on the brand's track record for every creator to see. Nobody is asked to pay for a video that never ran, which also means Residual isn't promising money it hasn't got. The compliance checklist is a gate with the brand's own rules on it, because in the markets I work in a video with the wrong legal footer can cost the advertiser its licence, and the person who approved it should be findable.

It is a prototype, and there is no funding behind it. That shapes the terms more than anything else on this page: Residual collects its fee before creators are paid rather than after, it never subsidises a payout, and the first revenue is the compliance desk, which is me doing by hand the thing the gate automates. The brand and creator screens work end to end on example data, and you can click through them above. Nothing is connected to a real ad account yet.

If you run the ads

You write a brief: the product, three to ten hooks for creators to pick from, the markets, and a pay rule. Percentage of spend, flat fee per approved video, or both, with an optional cap. The brief goes out to the creators in the pool who work your markets, and submissions start coming back in days rather than after the two to four weeks Trybe's own strategists budget for recruiting.

The checklist is the approval

Each submission arrives with the compliance checklist for its market and vertical already attached. Approve is locked until every item is ticked. If something is missing you send a revise, and the creator gets the exact item back rather than a note. Rejecting needs a reason from a fixed list. Every decision is logged with the reviewer's name, the date and the state of the checklist, so when compliance asks who passed a video and on what basis, the answer is one screen.

Terms written for the side that is harder to win

Brands are the scarce side of this market, so the terms lean your way wherever that doesn't break the creator's deal. Your first $10,000 of spend carries no platform fee, so the first brief is a free test. Approving costs you nothing: an approval you never run simply lapses, with 48 hours to withdraw before the clock even matters. Two free revisions per video. Once a creator has earned $250 from a video it's yours for good, paid and organic, on any platform. Want creators faster? Offer a kill fee, or something that costs you less than cash: merch, platform credit, a year of your premium tier. Residual takes nothing on it.

Launch, then let the sync do the bookkeeping

Attach the ad IDs the video runs under, as many as you like across ad sets and platforms. From then on each ad ID gets a ledger line per day with the spend the platform reported, the creator's share, and Residual's fee. Finance can reconcile it against Ads Manager without a spreadsheet. If you launch and then pause before $50 has run, the approval lapses all the same; that part protects the creator and costs you nothing but a line on your record.

If you make the videos

You see the pay rule before you claim a brief, worked out in money rather than percentages: what the brand spends per day, what a video carrying a third of that earns, where the cap sits. You see the checklist you will be judged against for each market, so you can film to it. Then you submit, and you are never guessing what the status means.

What it costs

Brands pay nothing until $10,000 of spend has run and 0.75% after that, debited weekly. Creators cover 5% of their earnings. There is no funding behind Residual, so nothing here is subsidised: no money leaves before it has arrived, which is how a one-person platform keeps a margin near 90% and still has no subscription and no minimum.
