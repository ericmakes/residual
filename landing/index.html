UGC ads where the creator is paid on ad spend they can see.

Brands pay creators a share of what they spend running the video. Residual pulls that spend from Meta, TikTok and Google every morning, line by line, and both sides read the same ledger.

Open the working prototypeRequest access

Free to start. Brands pay 0.75% of ad spend on performance rules, capped at $5,000 a month. Trybe charges 1.25% with no cap. Creators pay 2.5% of what they earn, so the platform's cost is shared instead of landing on the side that is harder to win.

Payouts go out every Friday through Stripe. If a brand approves a video and doesn't run it within 21 days, the creator is paid a kill fee; the brand has 48 hours after approving to change its mind at no cost.

No Shopify needed, which is the point. Apps, subscriptions, fintech and regulated advertisers have been locked out of this model so far.

Why this exists

A note from the person building it.

Trybe got one thing right and it is worth copying: brands pay creators on results rather than per video, and creators who make ads that run get paid for as long as they run. Five hundred of Trybe's brands pay a straight percentage of ad spend, most of them 10%, according to the rates Trybe publishes. That is a real market, and it is bigger than the Shopify stores Trybe built it for.

The problem is what the creator gets to see. A statement with a total on it. No spend, no ad IDs, no way to check the number against anything. And a video a brand approves but never bothers to launch earns nothing, which on a commission-only rule is a quiet way of getting free content. I run paid media for app and betting brands in Latin America, and no finance team I have worked with would pay a vendor invoice with that little behind it. Creators accept it because nobody has offered them better, at least so far.

So Residual pays on the number the ad platform reports for the ad IDs tied to the video, and shows the creator the same lines the brand sees. Approval starts a 21-day clock: run the video to $50 of spend or the creator gets a kill fee, with 48 hours after approving for the brand to withdraw at no cost. The compliance checklist is a gate with the brand's own rules on it, because in the markets I work in a video with the wrong legal footer can cost the advertiser its licence, and the person who approved it should be findable.

It is a prototype. The brand and creator screens work end to end on example data, and you can click through them above. Nothing is connected to a real ad account yet.

If you run the ads

You write a brief: the product, three to ten hooks for creators to pick from, the markets, and a pay rule. Percentage of spend, flat fee per approved video, or both, with an optional cap. The brief goes out to the creators in the pool who work your markets, and submissions start coming back in days rather than after the two to four weeks Trybe's own strategists budget for recruiting.

The checklist is the approval

Each submission arrives with the compliance checklist for its market and vertical already attached. Approve is locked until every item is ticked. If something is missing you send a revise, and the creator gets the exact item back rather than a note. Rejecting needs a reason from a fixed list. Every decision is logged with the reviewer's name, the date and the state of the checklist, so when compliance asks who passed a video and on what basis, the answer is one screen.

Terms written for the side that is harder to win

Brands are the scarce side of this market, so the terms lean your way where they can without breaking the creator's deal. You pay 0.75% rather than Trybe's 1.25%, and creators carry 2.5% of their own earnings. You get 48 hours after approving a video to withdraw with no kill fee, two revisions per video at no charge, and the clock is 21 days rather than 14. If a video turns into a winner you can buy out the creator's share for three times its trailing 30 days and run it as long as you like.

Launch, then let the sync do the bookkeeping

Attach the ad IDs the video runs under, as many as you like across ad sets and platforms. From then on each ad ID gets a ledger line per day with the spend the platform reported, the creator's share, and Residual's fee. Finance can reconcile it against Ads Manager without a spreadsheet. If you launch and then pause before $50 has run, the clock keeps going; that part protects the creator, and it is the one term that doesn't bend.

If you make the videos

You see the pay rule before you claim a brief, worked out in money rather than percentages: what the brand spends per day, what a video carrying a third of that earns, where the cap sits. You see the checklist you will be judged against for each market, so you can film to it. Then you submit, and you are never guessing what the status means.

Approved but not run
21 days, then the kill fee is yours. The brand can withdraw within the first 48 hours; after that the clock keys on spend, so pausing the ad straight after launch doesn't stop it.
Running
Yesterday's spend on each of your videos is in your ledger by 06:00, with the platform and the ad ID. If a line looks wrong, flag it and both sides see the flag.
Revise
The exact checklist item that failed, in writing. A new cut is re-checked from scratch.
Paid
Every Friday, through Stripe, in your country. Residual keeps 2.5% of what you earn and nothing from kill fees; Collabstr takes 15% from creators and JoinBrands 20%. Instant payout is there if you want it, at Stripe's cost with nothing added.

What it costs

Brands pay 40% less than on Trybe and creators cover part of the platform's cost. The two together leave about 78% gross margin after payout rails, which is why there is no subscription and no minimum.
